How it works

  1. 1

    Write the policy

    Sentences in a fixed template: an allowance limit, a contract allowlist, no full access keys, named methods only, and optional guard rules.

    The agent may spend up to 5 NEAR per day.
  2. 2

    Read the keys

    KeyProof queries the agent’s account and reads permission, allowance, receiver and method scope for every access key.

    view_access_key_list
  3. 3

    Read the verdict

    Each promise is marked holds, can be exceeded or unknown, with the key field that breaks it next to the sentence that promised otherwise.

    keys[1].allowance = 12 NEAR

Verify and enforce

KeyProof on its own reports. The guard contract is what stops the bad call. Together they close the gap between what you wrote and what the agent can do.

  1. 1

    The guard enforces

    A human owner funds a guard contract with a daily cap and a recipient allowlist. A call that breaks a rule reverts, and no NEAR moves.

    spend(to, amount_yocto)
  2. 2

    The agent’s key reaches only the guard

    The agent’s account holds one function call key that can call spend on the guard and nothing else. KeyProof checks its receiver, methods and permissions.

    receiver_id = "kp-guard.testnet"
  3. 3

    KeyProof reads the guard

    It calls the guard’s get_policy view and compares the cap, the recipients and the owner with the policy a human wrote.

    guard.daily_cap = 2 NEAR

Testnet only. The guard is not audited and only protects the money it holds.

Scope

Read only checker

The checker uses view calls only. Nothing is signed and no private key enters the app. The guard is a separate contract you deploy yourself.

Testnet, v1

No mainnet funds and no cross-chain intents. History, alerts and teams are out of scope.

Permissions, not judgment

KeyProof verifies key permissions, not whether the policy itself is sensible.
